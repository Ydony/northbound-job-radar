The introduction, before and after the first search

An introduction that is right on a first visit is wrong on a fiftieth. It shows in full until a search has completed on this account, then collapses to one line and stays there. Only a completed search collapses it — not a page load, not signing in, not pressing the button and having it fail. Until the product has done its job once, the explanation of what it does stays on screen.

A · No search has completed yet — 434px

Why this exists

“Ik ben een appel” is all the Dutch I know, so I built this to find the jobs that only need English.

I am learning Dutch slowly now, and eventually I will master it. But if you are like me, do use this to find yourself a job.

How to use it
01Say what you do

Up to five roles, one or two words each — analyst, supply chain. They are the only thing a search really needs.

02Press Find new jobs

It reads the public job boards for Switzerland and the Netherlands. It signs in to nothing and submits nothing on your behalf.

03Every advertisement is read

Each one is judged on language alone: definitely English, maybe English, not sure, or local language required.

04Open the ones worth opening

Apply on the employer’s own page, mark what you have applied for, and dismiss the rest. Dismissed is reversible.

What it does not do. It does not apply for anything, it holds no account on any job board, and it reads only pages anyone can open. A verdict is a reading of the text, not a guarantee — when it says Maybe English, it means it, and the original advertisement is one click away on every card.

What decides it

A completed search on this account, stored per account rather than per browser — so it does not come back on a second device, and clearing site data does not resurrect it. The nearest existing thing is the search_runs row the run already writes; a run that finished is the signal, not a new flag to keep in step with it.

The way back

“Read the rest” reopens the full band for that visit. It does not un-collapse permanently — someone who wants to re-read it wants to re-read it once, not to have a screen of explanation returned to them for good.

What this buys

366px. The first job moves from roughly 1180px to roughly 814px, which is the difference between scrolling past an explanation on every visit and not. The collapsed line still names the product’s reason, so the page never becomes anonymous.
